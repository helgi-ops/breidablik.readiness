MicroPulse — Today Command Center (whole page) — narration for Pictory
============================================================

Scene 1
Today Command Center — the whole page, explained in full. This is the most important page in MicroPulse: the one a coach opens every morning, and the only one he needs if all is well. Let's go through it in depth.

Scene 2
It's the command center. It reads every signal from every other page — load, readiness, injuries, tests — and gathers them here, returning a single decision per player: green, amber or red, and what to do. It's the one page the whole system exists to produce.

Scene 3
The top of the page is the verdict, in five seconds: one sentence — here, a rest day — with the confidence right beside it, twenty-four of twenty-five checked in. Four tiles split the squad into ready, modified and recovery, plus tomorrow's outlook. And an auto-lock thirty minutes before the session freezes the decision, so everyone sees the same answer. This is the demo squad — no real player data.

Scene 4
The whole page is built in three layers. The verdict — one colour and one sentence, with confidence, in five seconds. The plain why — two or three facts and the players to watch, visible without a click, in fifteen. And the detail — the per-player cards, the briefing and the S&C signals — on demand. It never jumps from verdict straight to raw data; the plain why always sits in between.

Scene 5
Right under the verdict is Needs attention — the two or three players who actually need a decision today, marked alert or watch, so your attention goes there first rather than scanning the whole squad. And on a green day it's short or empty, which is itself the message: carry on as planned.

Scene 6
Click a player and his whole story opens in a drawer. The why — in plain language, here that yesterday's load was sixty-five per cent above usual, with the numbers behind it. The counterfactual — what would have to change to flip the verdict: if readiness had been green, not red, this would be yellow. And an AI summary, labelled as AI, alongside the confidence — four of five signals. It's the layered read for one player, the whole manifesto in one panel.

Scene 7
Below that sits the Decision Summary — one card per player, with nothing hidden. The trend from yesterday, whether he's improving or sharply declining. And the decision, with the numbers that produced it: the wellness scores — sleep, soreness, energy, mood — and the load line — distance, body load, high-speed, sprint, accelerations, braking. Everything that drove the call is right there on the card.

Scene 8
Open Show details and you get the Daily Briefing — an auto-generated morning view you can send to staff. A team pulse: is the squad improving or declining versus yesterday, the average readiness, the fatigue mix. The top attention players with a plain why and an Ask-AI button. And compliance — how many checked in, how many RPEs, and who's missing. It's the morning summary in one place.

Scene 9
Beneath that is the S&C layer — unfamiliar load and S&C signals. Recovery watch, for players still below baseline after a match, with an escalate action — that's the return-to-training integration. Spiking alerts, for who's carrying a load or high-speed spike, linking straight to the Load and IMA pages. And moving differently — who's moving unlike his usual, framed explicitly as a descriptive signal, not an injury prediction. It weaves the other pages into the daily view.

Scene 10
And this is the big idea. Today is the aggregator — it surfaces the key signals from Load Intelligence, Decel, IMA, Post-match Recovery and return-to-training, so the coach doesn't have to open each one. The decision lives here; the depth is one click away when he wants the why. That's why the system can look complex but really be one page — all the others are simply its depth.

Scene 11
It's worth naming why Today feels trustworthy. Every rule the product is built on is visible on this one screen. The layered read — verdict to why to detail. Every verdict shows its confidence. Missing data is shown as missing. Every flag carries a counterfactual. And the AI labels itself as AI and cites its signals. Rules decide, the AI explains — and you can see all of it. Today is the manifesto, on one screen.

Scene 12
So, thirty seconds. First, the verdict and the confidence — if it's all green, that's all you need. Second, Needs attention — the two or three players to look at. Third, open a flagged player for the why and the counterfactual in his drawer. And everything else — the cards, the briefing, the S&C signals — is one toggle away.

Scene 13
The bottom line: Today answers what to do today, per player, with the why. The reasoning, the confidence and the S&C depth are all reachable, but never in the way. It's the page a coach lives on — and the page everything else in MicroPulse serves.
