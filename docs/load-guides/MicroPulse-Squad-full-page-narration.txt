MicroPulse — Squad (S&C surface) — narration for Pictory
============================================================

Scene 1
The Squad page, explained in full — the S&C surface. This is the full per-player drill-down behind Today's head-coach verdict. Let's walk through it.

Scene 2
It's the S&C surface. For each player, it lays out every engine's output — the readiness decision, injury risk, external load, fatigue, the auto-planned session and neural load — each with its own why, confidence and coach action. Every engine is open, and the coach can audit it all, and override the call, with the change logged.

Scene 3
At the top are the day's flags — low readiness, pain, neural bias applied — the team-level attention. You can filter by colour, and re-run the engine. And for each player there are three buttons — full, reduced, recovery — so the coach can override the engine's call, logged with a reason. The engine advises; the coach decides.

Scene 4
Each player has a card — the head-coach read before you drill in. The status, like yellow eighteen, with a plain why. And three tiles: today, his z-score versus his own usual; versus yesterday, the change overnight; and a rating on a zero-to-ten scale where five is the squad average. Then Show details opens the workbench.

Scene 5
The readiness decision explains itself. A why — PlayerLoad elevated versus the twenty-eight-day baseline, deceleration and acceleration above norm, rising neural load. And a coach action — protect painful tissue, reduce external load, cap intensity. Plus a tomorrow's outlook: if he improves it's yellow, if it holds it stays red — and honestly, when history is thin, it says forecast confidence is low.

Scene 6
Beside readiness sits injury risk — a separate engine, with its own why and recommendation. Here, fatigue remains elevated while recovery markers are below normal, so it recommends controlling high-speed and explosive volume, modifying total load, monitoring the response in warm-up, and prioritising recovery. Two engines, each with its own reasoning — they don't collapse into one number.

Scene 7
And you can see the raw check-in — nothing hidden. The player's actual wellness scores: fatigue and energy, sleep quality and duration, stress and mood, muscle soreness. With the baseline maturity, when it was logged, and the supporting z-scores. So a coach can see exactly what objective input drove the verdict, rather than trusting a black box.

Scene 8
The external load section shows two things. Today versus the team — where his load sits against the squad right now, often all green. And the weekly load: seven-day, twenty-eight-day, and the ACWR per metric, with the spiking ones flagged red. Here total accelerations and high-intensity distance are spiking, even though today looked green. Both the snapshot and the rolling ratio are shown, because they can disagree.

Scene 9
The fatigue and adaptation section is where a flag becomes an action. It names the type — here tissue fatigue, moderate, driven by high deceleration and acceleration load yesterday. And then it prescribes concrete modifiers: minus forty per cent volume, a recovery bias, swap the ballistic elements, add tendon reload, and a neural bias of minus eight per cent. The engine doesn't just flag fatigue — it turns it into an adapted session.

Scene 10
Finally, two more engines. The auto session — a reset or recovery plan, with its decision confidence: the inputs it used, what was missing, and any fallbacks, plus the actual warm-up and isometrics. And neural load — a separate engine tracking rising or declining neural fatigue and next-day risk, with the bias it applied to the decision. Every engine states its inputs and its confidence, so you can see exactly how the final call was built.

Scene 11
It's worth stepping back. Today is the head-coach surface — the verdict, in five seconds. Squad is the S&C surface — the whole workbench, every engine open. Same truth, two depths. The head coach gets the answer on Today; the S&C coach gets the full reasoning here; and both can override with a logged reason. One verdict, visible everywhere — and the reasoning lives on Squad.

Scene 12
So, thirty seconds. First, the player card's status and why. Second, the readiness decision — the why and the coach action. And if you disagree, override with full, reduced or recovery, logged. Everything else — injury risk, the weekly ACWR flags, the adapted session, the neural engine — is there when you need it. Every engine shows its reasoning and its confidence, and the coach decides. Squad is the depth that Today simplifies.
