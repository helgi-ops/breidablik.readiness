MicroPulse — The Today Page: How to Use (updated) — narration for Pictory
============================================================

Scene 1
The Today page — how to use it. The page has grown, so here's the updated walkthrough. It's your morning cockpit: one screen that takes you from the shape of the whole day down to a single decision for one player. We'll go top to bottom, in the order the page reads.

Scene 2
The whole page is built to answer four questions, in order. Outlook — how's the team? Who — who needs me today? What — what should they actually do? And why — what's behind it? Each section down the page answers the next question, so you never have to scan all twenty-five players to find the one who needs you. Follow the page and you follow the workflow.

Scene 3
Section one, at the very top: the Command Center. It's the shape of the day before you read a single name. The team outlook — one word, Ready, Caution, or High. The split — a live count of how many are Ready, Modified, or Recovery, in the same green, amber, and red as everywhere else. And the actions plus tomorrow — the top things to do this morning, and a look-ahead to the next day. Read the strip and you know your morning.

Scene 4
Section two: Needs Attention — who. It's the ranked short list of players to look at; everyone who's fine simply isn't on it. It's grouped worst-on-top — alert, rehab, injured, watch — with the urgent groups expanded and the rest collapsed to keep it short. Each row shows change before state — the overnight drop, down-down worse, which is what triggers action rather than the colour alone. And injury always leads: an injured player is on the list even if his wellness reads green.

Scene 5
Section three: Decision Summary — what. This is where every signal becomes one training decision per player, for today: Full, Modified, Recovery, or Off — an action, not a number. Rules decide and the AI only explains: the decision comes from the engine and the canonical readiness colour, and the written summary just narrates, labelled as AI. And a safety net of hard overrides — injury, off day, post-match, and a flag when the plan says Full but the player's own STEN says otherwise.

Scene 6
Section four: Unfamiliar Load and the S and C signals — why. This is the context behind the decisions, and it's descriptive, never an injury prediction. Recovery watch — who's still below baseline the day after a game. Load and movement spikes — where squad load is climbing and who did unusual high-intensity work. And Unfamiliar Load — who's moving differently than his own norm, so you can phase the new demand in rather than pile it on.

Scene 7
Section five, at the bottom behind Show details: the full Daily Briefing — the complete morning read, answer-first. The headline verdict — one sentence: fresh, caution, risk, or rest, and who to watch. The tiles and team pulse — the readiness split, the planned day, the load, and which way the squad is trending. And the top-attention players inline, each with an action, a plain-language reason, and its citation. It's the whole day, laid out in full for when you want it.

Scene 8
One thing works the same everywhere: wherever a player appears — in Needs Attention, in the Decision Summary, in the briefing — tapping the row opens the same drawer. And the drawer is the layered read, all the way down: the verdict, the plain-language why, the counterfactual — if this one thing had been different, he'd be green — the confidence, the underlying signals with their citation, and the AI summary, clearly labelled as AI. The list triages; the drawer explains.

Scene 9
And underneath all five sections run the same four rules. Everyone is judged against himself — every verdict is the player against his own baseline. Rules decide and the AI only explains — the rules set the verdict, the AI narrates and cites its signals but never decides, and it's always labelled. One verdict everywhere — the colour is identical on every surface, from one canonical source. And honest confidence — thin or estimated data is labelled, never dressed up as fine. Those four rules are what make the whole page trustworthy.

Scene 10
So, thirty seconds to use the whole page. First, read the strip — the Command Center, the outlook and the Ready-Modified-Recovery split. Second, work the list — Needs Attention for who, Decision Summary for what. Third, open any row for the why — the drawer with the verdict, counterfactual, confidence, and citation. Outlook, who, what, why; everyone against his own norm; rules decide and the AI explains; and one verdict everywhere. That's the Today page.
