# Teaching guide — Contextualised peak periods (how to teach it)

*A ready-to-deliver session for coaches / S&C staff. ~45–60 minutes. Written for you to present; can be
delivered in Icelandic. Pairs with two visuals you already have:
`contextualised-peak-periods-page.html` (the taxonomy + the position chart) and
`fusion-oli-valur-visual.html` (the real Breiðablik example).*

## Who this is for and what they should leave with

Audience: football (or basketball) coaches and fitness/S&C staff who already know what a "peak period"
is from GPS but treat it as a single number.

By the end they should be able to:
1. Explain why a peak period is not just a distance — it has tactical content.
2. Name the dominant actions in a peak period and how they differ by position.
3. Read a physical-peak-×-tactical overlay and say what it means for that player.
4. Turn a peak-demand number into a position-specific drill decision.

Keep one honest thread running through the whole session: **this is a lens for training, never a verdict
on a player.**

## The one sentence to anchor everything

Write it on the board first and come back to it at the end:

> *"GPS tells you how hard he ran. Only the tactical context tells you what that running was — and
> therefore how to train it."*

## Session flow

### 1. Hook (5 min) — the two-peaks story
Don't start with theory. Start with the real example. Put up the Óli Valur visual and ask the room:
*"Same player, one match. Here are his two hardest spells. One minute, one five-minute run. What was he
doing?"* Let them guess. Then reveal: the hardest minute was **defending a corner** (interception +
clearance); the hardest run was **his own attack** down the wing (two crosses, a shot). Opposite
meanings. Ask: *"If you'd only seen the GPS numbers, which drill would you have built?"* That tension is
the whole session.

### 2. The concept (8 min) — why the number isn't enough
Define a peak period (hardest rolling 1/3/5 min). Then the key research finding (Ju et al. 2022, 50 EPL
matches, 350 hours of video coding): peak periods are dominated by **Recovery Run + Covering** out of
possession and **Support Play** in possession — *not* attacking sprints. Land the implication: a drill
that only rehearses attacking runs under-prepares players for their actual hardest minutes.

### 3. The taxonomy (10 min) — the shared language
Open the contextualised-peak-periods page. Walk the three groups: In Possession (Push up Pitch, Break
into Box, Run in Behind, Over/Underlap, Run with Ball, Move to Receive, Support Play), Out of Possession
(Interception, Recovery Run, Covering, Close Down/Press), Unclassifiable. Keep it quick — you're giving
them vocabulary, not a memory test. Then the position chart: click through centre-back → wide defender →
midfield → wide attacker → striker and read out the headline for each ("the centre-back's peak is ~80%
out of possession, covering and recovering"). The takeaway is **position-specific**, not one-size-fits-all.

### 4. The MicroPulse overlay (10 min) — how we do it without a video analyst
Be honest and specific. Two feeds on the same clock: the physical peak window from Catapult, and the
tactical events from the match. Where you don't have coded events, the **IMA directional signature**
(forward / backward / lateral) approximates the same question automatically on every session — forward +
high HSR = attacking; backward + heavy braking = defensive transition. Note the HSR threshold is
>19.8 km/h (Velocity Band 5), the same as the research. State the limits out loud (this builds
credibility with an expert room): on-ball events only, off-ball patterns aren't in the feed, and it
never touches the readiness verdict.

### 5. Application (10 min) — from number to drill
This is where coaches switch on. Give them the position map and make it concrete:
- **Centre-back** → repeated covering + recovery-run efforts (defensive transition).
- **Wide defender** → recovery + overlap/underlap + support play.
- **Midfielder** → transition support play (the most Support Play of any position).
- **Wide attacker** → move-to-receive + run-with-ball + run-in-behind.
- **Striker** → press / close-down + run-in-behind.

Then the two periodisation points: **pre-season** you build the ceiling and progress toward and past the
peak demand; **in-season** you replicate peak *episodes* in small-sided/positional games, top up players
who played under 30 minutes, and train the *repeat-after-peak* quality (the minute after the peak drops
most). Stress the benchmark is a **season curve**, not one match — one game is noise.

### 6. Activity (7 min) — make them do it
Give each small group one position and one prompt: *"Design a 10-minute drill that loads this position's
real peak content."* Two groups share back. Correct gently toward the tactical content (a centre-back
drill that's all straight-line sprints misses the point; it should have covering and recovery under a
turnover).

### 7. Close (3 min)
Back to the anchor sentence. One-line summary: *"A peak period is a decision, not a distance."* Point them
to where they'll see it in the system.

## Talking points that land

- "The hardest minute of the game is usually defensive. That surprises people — use it."
- "You're not training players to run more. You're training them to do the *right kind* of running under
  the *right kind* of pressure."
- "The number is the same for a full-back and a striker; the *content* is completely different. That's
  the whole argument for position-specific loading."
- Honesty is a feature here: "We approximate the tactical read from movement and on-ball events — we
  don't pretend to reproduce a video analyst's hand-coding."

## Questions you'll get (and honest answers)

- *"Isn't this just what my analyst already does?"* — Your analyst codes video after the game. This puts
  the tactical content on the same clock as the **physical** peak, automatically, so you can act on it in
  training — and it runs every match, not just the ones someone had time to code.
- *"Do I need Wyscout/StatsBomb for every game?"* — No. The physical benchmark (HSR, IMA, peak windows)
  is automatic from Catapult. The event overlay is only for matches you want the tactical story on — a
  handful, e.g. one per position.
- *"What about players with no GPS / a basketball squad?"* — The taxonomy and the training logic still
  apply; you lose the physical overlay but keep the position-specific principle.
- *"Can I trust one match?"* — No, and don't. Use the season curve. One game shows the concept; the
  benchmark is the rolling last 8–12 weeks.
