# Validation plan — does anyone (but Breiðablik) pay?

> **The one question this answers:** will a club other than your own pay real
> money for MicroPulse? Everything else (more features, the GTM plan, the
> declutter) is downstream of this. Until a stranger pays, demand is unproven.
>
> Companion to `MicroPulse-borgud-prufa-tilbod.pdf` (the offer you hand over) and
> `MicroPulse-utbreidsluplan-Catapult-Island.pdf` (the market map).

## The trap to avoid

162 features are built and exactly one club uses it — your own. That is building
ahead of selling. **Stop adding features until one stranger pays.** A free pilot
does not count: it tells you the product is nice, not that it's worth money. Only
a paid pilot validates willingness-to-pay.

## Who to talk to (two different people)

- **The champion / user** — head coach or performance/S&C lead. Feels the pain,
  will love the product, but usually does NOT control budget.
- **The budget owner** — framkvæmdastjóri / stjórn / sporting director. You need
  BOTH: the champion to pull you in, the budget owner to say yes to money. A
  common failure is a thrilled coach and no budget line. Ask the champion early:
  "who would have to approve a few hundred thousand króna for this?"

## Discovery questions (ask, don't pitch — first half of every meeting)

1. Who looks at your Catapult data today, and how much time do they actually have?
2. What decisions do you make from it now? What gets ignored because no one has time?
3. Last hamstring/soft-tissue injury — did the load data warn you? Did anyone see it?
4. Is there a budget line for performance/analysis software? Who owns it?
5. What would have to be true for you to pay for something like this next season?

If they can't name a person who does the analysis, or admit data goes unused —
that's your wedge, said in their words. Use it back to them.

## What counts as a real YES vs NO

- **Real YES** = a signed paid pilot (even small) with a start date and a named
  contact. Money changed hands or is committed in writing.
- **Soft signals that are NOT a yes:** "looks great", "send me info", "let's stay
  in touch", a free trial. Treat these as a no until money is committed.
- **Clean NO (also valuable):** no budget owner reachable, no analysis pain, or
  "only if it's free." A fast no lets you move to the next club.

## The sprint (≈6 weeks)

1. **Week 1:** finish the Breiðablik case study (1 page: real numbers + a coach
   quote + the Anton unfamiliar-load catch). This is your proof.
2. **Weeks 2–5:** take the paid-pilot offer to the 3 Pro S7 clubs (Stjarnan, HK,
   ÍA). Run discovery first, then present the offer. Target: **1 paid pilot.**
3. **Week 6:** decide based on evidence.
   - 1+ paid pilot → demand is real; proceed to Phase 2 of the GTM plan.
   - 3 clubs, zero willing to pay → the product is good but the business needs a
     rethink (price? buyer? Iceland-only vs international? Catapult-dependency?).
     Better to learn this now than after building feature #200.

## Pricing — to decide (your internal note, not for the club sheet)

- Charge per **club** (both men's + women's), not per team — doubles value per sale.
- Tier the price to the Catapult tier (Pro / Core / Lengjudeild) — value follows
  data depth.
- Pilot should be paid but low-friction; the real number is the ongoing
  subscription. Set both before the first Pro meeting and write them into the
  `[VERÐ]` / `[mánaðar-/árgjald]` placeholders in the offer PDF.
- Reality check: Icelandic clubs are not rich, Lengjudeild least of all. Anchor on
  "less than the cost of one analyst, more than free." Validate the number in the
  discovery calls (question 5) before locking it.

## Strategic question to hold in the back of your mind

Iceland is ~17 clubs. Full capture is a modest business, not a startup. Decide
honestly whether the ambition is (a) a sustainable Iceland-focused product, or
(b) something that scales to other Catapult markets (Scandinavia, lower-league
UK). If (b), keep the core product less Iceland-specific and treat
Catapult-platform dependency as the #1 strategic risk to de-risk early.
