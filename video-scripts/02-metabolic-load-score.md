# Script 2 — Metabolic Load Score

**Length:** ~2 min 30 sec voiceover (~400 words)
**Audience:** Coaches, S&C staff, sports medicine
**Tool:** Pictory (paste the voiceover blocks; use the visual cues as image / B-roll prompts)

---

## SCENE 1 — Hook (0:00–0:15)

**Visual:** A footballer running at varying speeds — burst, slow, burst, slow. Overlaid energy-meter ticks rapidly. Cut to a flatline jog that barely moves the meter.

**Narration:**
A constant jog burns calories. A constant jog with eight sudden bursts burns three times more. But your stopwatch tells you the same total time. Metabolic Load Score is what tells you the real cost.

---

## SCENE 2 — Definition (0:15–1:00)

**Visual:** Animated formula appears, then expands:
*Metabolic Load Score = energy expenditure derived from velocity changes × time, expressed in watts per kilogram*

**Narration:**
Metabolic Load Score measures how much energy an athlete actually spent — based on how often, and how sharply, their speed changed.

Originally developed by Italian sports scientist Pietro di Prampero, the formula treats running with acceleration as equivalent to running uphill. The harder you accelerate, the steeper the imaginary slope.

It's expressed in watts per kilogram — the same unit used in cycling. A typical training session might score around 8 to 12 watts per kilo. A high-intensity match can exceed 25.

---

## SCENE 3 — Why it matters (1:00–1:45)

**Visual:** Split screen. Left: athlete on a treadmill at constant speed — heart rate steady. Right: athlete in small-sided game with constant speed changes — heart rate spiking irregularly. A counter shows energy expenditure climbing 60% faster on the right.

**Narration:**
Two sessions. Same average speed. Same total distance. Right-hand session burns twice the energy. The difference is the speed changes — every burst, every cut, every sudden brake costs metabolic fuel even if it doesn't add a single metre to the distance counter.

This is why average heart rate is a poor measure of session intensity in team sport. Heart rate lags. Metabolic Load Score doesn't. It captures every spike, every nuance of variable-pace work — exactly the way football is actually played.

---

## SCENE 4 — How MicroPulse uses it (1:45–2:10)

**Visual:** MicroPulse coach view shows a session summary card. "Metabolic Power avg: 14.2 W/kg — 1.1 SD above baseline" with a yellow flag.

**Narration:**
MicroPulse computes Metabolic Load Score for every session, every player. We baseline each athlete against their own personal twenty-eight-day rolling history. So a striker's normal load isn't compared to a defender's normal — it's compared to *their* normal.

When the score drifts above the player's baseline band, the coach sees it before they plan the next session.

---

## SCENE 5 — Practical takeaway (2:10–2:30)

**Visual:** Comparison panel: Player A — high distance, low metabolic. Player B — low distance, high metabolic. Coach annotation: "Player B needs the recovery."

**Narration:**
Distance tells you how far. Metabolic Load Score tells you how *expensive* it was. For variable-intensity sports like football, metabolic load is what fatigues the engine — and what determines how much real recovery the next twenty-four hours need to deliver.
