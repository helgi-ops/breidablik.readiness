# Script 4 — Composite Load

**Length:** ~2 min 30 sec voiceover (~400 words)
**Audience:** Coaches, S&C staff, sports medicine
**Tool:** Pictory (paste the voiceover blocks; use the visual cues as image / B-roll prompts)

---

## SCENE 1 — Hook (0:00–0:15)

**Visual:** A coach overwhelmed at a desk with twenty different graphs — distance, heart rate, accelerations, sprints, RPE, sleep score. Slide transition: all twenty graphs collapse into a single number. "Today: 87."

**Narration:**
Every GPS unit gives you twenty different numbers. Every wellness questionnaire gives you another five. No coach has time to read them all. Composite Load gives you one.

---

## SCENE 2 — Definition (0:15–1:00)

**Visual:** Animated formula appears layer by layer:
*Composite Load = Mechanical Load × 0.35 + Metabolic Load × 0.35 + Neuromuscular Burden × 0.30*

**Narration:**
Composite Load is the unifying number — a single index that combines the three load systems we've already covered: mechanical, metabolic, and neuromuscular.

The weighting reflects what sports science research tells us about injury risk and performance decrement. Mechanical work — the stop-start, the cuts, the braking — gets thirty-five percent. Metabolic work — the energy spent on variable-pace running — also gets thirty-five percent. Neuromuscular burden — the fast-twitch demand — gets thirty percent.

The result is a single value, scaled so that one is "a typical training day" for this athlete and two is "as hard as a competitive match."

---

## SCENE 3 — Why it matters (1:00–1:45)

**Visual:** Animation of the ACWR concept. Two bars: a seven-day Acute load on the left, twenty-eight-day Chronic load on the right. A ratio is calculated — 1.3 — and labelled "sweet spot." Then the acute bar grows; ratio jumps to 1.8; alarm bell rings labelled "injury risk doubles."

**Narration:**
Composite Load isn't just a daily summary. It powers the most important number in modern athlete monitoring: the Acute-to-Chronic Workload Ratio.

The research, led by Tim Gabbett and replicated dozens of times since, shows that injury risk roughly doubles when an athlete's recent seven-day load exceeds their preceding four-week load by more than fifty percent.

You can't compute that ratio meaningfully from raw distance — different sessions have different intensities. You need a single, intensity-weighted number. That's Composite Load.

---

## SCENE 4 — How MicroPulse uses it (1:45–2:10)

**Visual:** MicroPulse team dashboard. Twenty player rows, each with a green / yellow / red Composite Load badge for today, and a small ACWR sparkline beside it. One player flashes red — "ACWR 1.7 — recovery day recommended."

**Narration:**
On the MicroPulse dashboard, every player's Composite Load is computed nightly. Every player's seven-day-to-twenty-eight-day ratio is tracked. When the ratio drifts into the "danger zone" — above 1.5 — that player's row turns red, with a recommended training modification.

The coach plans the week in five minutes. Not five hours.

---

## SCENE 5 — Practical takeaway (2:10–2:30)

**Visual:** Coach taps a red player card. Recommendation: "Reduce session volume by 30% · move neuromuscular work to Thursday." The card returns to green for tomorrow.

**Narration:**
Composite Load is the answer to "how hard was today, really?" — and the input to "what should tomorrow look like?" One number, three load systems, every athlete, every day.
