# Script 1 — Mechanical Load Index

**Length:** ~2 min 30 sec voiceover (~400 words)
**Audience:** Coaches, S&C staff, sports medicine
**Tool:** Pictory (paste the voiceover blocks; use the visual cues as image / B-roll prompts)

---

## SCENE 1 — Hook (0:00–0:15)

**Visual:** Slow-motion clip of a footballer planting hard, changing direction. Cut to GPS data overlay showing a distance graph that *looks* low.

**Narration:**
A player can run six kilometres in a session and walk off feeling fresh. Another player runs the same six kilometres and is wrecked the next morning. Total distance does not explain the difference. Mechanical Load Index does.

---

## SCENE 2 — Definition (0:15–0:55)

**Visual:** On-screen formula appears, animated piece by piece:
*Mechanical Load Index = Accelerations × intensity weight + Decelerations × intensity weight + Change-of-direction events*

**Narration:**
Mechanical Load Index, or M-L-I, is a composite of every explosive mechanical action an athlete does in a session. Not their steady running — that's metabolic. Mechanical load is the start-stop, plant-and-cut, brake-and-burst work that loads tendons, ligaments, and bone.

It combines three things: accelerations, decelerations, and change-of-direction events. Each one is weighted by how intense it was. A maximum-effort sprint start counts more than a jog-to-walk transition. A hard 90-degree cut counts more than a soft arc.

---

## SCENE 3 — Why it matters (0:55–1:35)

**Visual:** Side-by-side video — left side: a midfielder doing repeated pressing actions, lots of decels. Right side: a centre-back jogging at a steady pace. Distance counter rises equally on both sides; a "Mechanical Load" gauge spikes way higher on the left.

**Narration:**
Two players. Same distance. Same duration. Wildly different mechanical load. That is the heart of why M-L-I exists.

Sports-science research consistently shows that *eccentric* mechanical work — the braking, the cutting, the landing — is the strongest predictor of soft-tissue and joint injury. Quads, patellar tendon, ACL, calves, ankles — and hamstrings on the *sprint* side, not the braking side. Not how far you ran. How hard you stopped.

If a coach only sees total distance, they undercook recovery for the press-heavy midfielder and overcook it for the centre-back. M-L-I fixes that.

---

## SCENE 4 — How MicroPulse uses it (1:35–2:05)

**Visual:** Screen recording of the MicroPulse coach dashboard. A player card highlights with "Mechanical Load: 1,420 — 1.4 SD above baseline". The colour shifts from green to yellow.

**Narration:**
Inside MicroPulse, every athlete has their own personal Mechanical Load baseline — built from the last twenty-eight days of their own data, not a league average. So a midfielder's normal might be twelve hundred. A goalkeeper's normal might be three hundred.

When today's M-L-I drifts more than one standard deviation above baseline, the player flags yellow. More than two standard deviations, red. The coach sees this before the next session starts.

---

## SCENE 5 — Practical takeaway (2:05–2:30)

**Visual:** A coach tablet view showing three players. Two green, one yellow. The yellow player's card opens — recommended action: "Reduce eccentric load · skip pressing drills · technical session only."

**Narration:**
Mechanical Load Index turns "the player feels tired" into "the player did 1.4 standard deviations more eccentric work than usual — and here's what to change tomorrow."

That's the difference between guessing about recovery, and managing it.
